## Why Marbella is Europe's Padel Capital

Marbella earned its title as Europe's padel capital through a perfect storm of history, geography, and culture. In 1974, Alfonso de Hohenlohe—an Andalusian aristocrat and entrepreneur—brought the first padel court to Europe at the legendary Marbella Club. What began as an exotic curiosity among the Costa del Sol's elite quickly became a phenomenon. The sport's accessibility, compared to tennis, meant it spread rapidly through Marbella's cosmopolitan expat community and wealthy Spanish clientele alike.

The climate is undeniably a factor too: Marbella enjoys over 300 days of sunshine annually, making year-round padel possible. This consistency drew professional players, coaches, and padel academies to establish their bases here. Today, Marbella hosts more than 40 dedicated padel facilities, from boutique clubs to sprawling complexes. The infrastructure—combined with the sport's cultural significance and the town's reputation as a playground for European elites—has cemented Marbella's position. It's not just where padel began in Europe; it's where the sport's identity was forged.
